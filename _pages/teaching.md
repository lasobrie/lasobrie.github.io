My teaching philosophy is hands-on and user-centric: I aspire to teach through building and testing models in real contexts, then reflecting on trade-offs, ethics, and impact. I aim to develop clear thinkers and careful builders who can translate analytics into safe, people-centered decisions.

## Course Involvement
<p>
  <strong>Managerial Economics, Guest Lecture, IESEG School of Management, France  (Spring 2024).</strong><br>
  <em>Prof. Dr. Marijn Verschelde</em>
</p>
<p>
  Half-day guest lecture on <em>Generative AI</em> (GenAI) and the future of work. I covered (i) core GenAI technologies, (ii) its multimodal capabilities, (iii) benefits, risks, and limitations, and (iv) expected labor-market impacts and adaptation strategies. Students presented their business ideas related to (Gen)AI; and we conducted a group discussion on the market potential of their idea using a SWOT-like framework.
</p>

<p>
  <strong>AI for Systems Engineering, Virginia Tech, USA (Fall 2024).</strong><br>
  <em>Prof. Dr. Joseph Godfrey</em>
</p>
<p>
    Lecture on deep learning from a systems-engineering perspective, including fundamentals, modern architectures, and the connection between DL and GenAI. We also discussed ethics, deployment considerations, and case studies illustrating practical applications. The students gained insights into the technical driver behind AI from an engineering perspective, as well as a more business-like understanding of how AI can be translated to operational context while taking ethics and safety into account.
</p>

  <em>Prof. Dr. Konstantinos P. Triantis</em>
</p>
<p>
  Provided course support and project guidance on socio-technical systems in the PhD level course, integrating management theory with engineering practice. Emphasis was placed on problem framing, data-driven analysis, and translating results into actionable recommendations for complex technical organizations. I specifically assist multiple groups in framing the research problem (the why), the modeling setup (the how), the most unique insights (the what, when), and the managerial implications (the so what).

<p>
  <strong>Vibrant Virginia: The Future of Next: Empowering the Youth in Decision Science and Human-Machine Interactions through STEMkits  (Fall 2025).</strong><br>
  <em>Dr. Maria Tomai</em>
</p>
<p>
  Active contributor to this K–12 program in Virginia that introduces students to Decision Science and Human–Machine Interaction through learning objectives and hands-on activities tailored to the respective age groups. The curriculum blends hardware (drones, model trains, tracks, cameras) with software (video, programming languages, introductory AI) to explore decision-making in transportation systems. Students investigate how people and machines collaborate, then apply concepts in guided experiments and mini-projects. This initiative is a collaboration with the Immersive 3D Lab, K12 Lab, and Drone Lab at Virginia Tech’s Academic Building 1 in Alexandria.
</p>
